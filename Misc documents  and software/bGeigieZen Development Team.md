# bGeigieZen Development Team

Based on my research, I've identified the core development team behind the bGeigieZen project. The team consists of **10 key contributors** from diverse backgrounds and locations, working collaboratively on this open-source citizen science project.

## Core Development Team

**Rob Oudendijk** - **Lead Developer and Hardware/Software Engineer**

- **Organization**: YR-Design / Safecast
- **Location**: Nara, Japan
- **Key Role**: Leading the bGeigieZen design team, responsible for overall hardware and software development[^1][^2][^3]
- **Background**: CEO of YR-Design with over 11 years of experience with Safecast, electrical engineering background from HTS Amsterdam[^1]

**Jelle Bouwhuis** - **Hardware Developer**

- **Organization**: Safecast
- **Location**: Netherlands
- **Key Role**: Hardware development and testing[^3]

**Louis Bertrand** - **Software Developer and GPS Specialist**

- **Organization**: Independent Contributor
- **Location**: Ontario, Canada
- **Key Role**: Development of GPS logging features, software development, testing and validation[^6][^7]
- **Background**: Retired electrical engineer, software developer, and college educator actively involved in nuclear monitoring advocacy[^7]

**Michael Beakes** - **GPS Development Specialist**

- **Organization**: U.S. Environmental Protection Agency
- **Location**: Sacramento, California, USA
- **Key Role**: GPS accuracy improvements, aviation mode development, and location tracking optimization[^8][^6]

## Team Structure and Collaboration

The bGeigieZen development team operates as a **distributed, international collaboration** with members across multiple continents. The team structure reflects Safecast's open-source philosophy, combining:

- **Professional expertise** from industry veterans (Ray Ozzie's Microsoft background, Rob Oudendijk's engineering experience)
- **Academic contributions** from researchers and educators (Michael Beakes' EPA work, Louis Bertrand's engineering background)
- **Specialized skills** in hardware design, software development, and citizen science
- **Geographic diversity** spanning Japan, USA, Canada, Netherlands, and Hong Kong

The development process is coordinated through GitHub repositories, Google Groups discussions, and the \#bgeigiezen Slack channel. This collaborative approach has enabled the team to successfully transition from the bGeigieNano to the more advanced bGeigieZen platform while maintaining the open-source principles that define Safecast's mission.[^3]

The team's multidisciplinary approach, combining expertise in electronics, software engineering, environmental science, and citizen science methodology, has been crucial to addressing the complex technical challenges involved in creating a user-friendly, accurate, and accessible radiation monitoring device for global citizen science applications.
